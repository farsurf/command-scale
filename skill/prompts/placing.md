You are given ONE closed task: their messages in order, each followed by what
came back after it. Some of the messages carry
a note saying they were not input at all, put there by a step that read the
conversation and never saw this ladder.

You place their messages on a ladder. You are not told what rung they are on,
you cannot see any running total, and you are not asked for an opinion about
them.

## The standards

The rungs are ordered, not exclusive. A message belongs at the highest rung
whose bar it clears, and everything a lower rung closes is closed at every rung
above it. Where a rung says something is "still open", that describes that
rung's own case; it is never a requirement on the rungs above it.

The whole ladder measures one thing: **how far past what already exists can
they get the assistant to go.**

At the bottom, the only things that come out right are things the assistant has
built many times before — the request is high-level enough that everything
about the result is supplied from what that name usually means. Climbing, the
person supplies more and the assistant supplies less, until what comes out is
theirs rather than a remembered pattern. At the top they are choosing what to
specify at all: leaving alone whatever the assistant already knows, spending
words only where it does not, and breaking the unfamiliar down until every
piece is something familiar.

A useful test at every rung: **hand this message to two different assistants —
how far apart would the two results be?** Far apart means the priors are doing
the work. Converging means the person is.

Every rung in every column measures ONE thing: **how much designing is left to
whoever acts on the message.** Not how large the thing is, not how hard it
would be to build, not how original it was, not how long the message is. Ask
only what is now closed and what is still open, and place it where the boundary
falls.

---

**asking** — the part of a message that says what should become true. It
covers whatever is being made, and equally the work itself and whatever they
want handed back to them.

Every placement in this column, at every rung including the first, must carry
`becomes`: what would exist, or be different, once that part is acted on. The
thing being made. The way the work is to proceed from here. The thing to be
handed over — something handed over exists once it has been. At the first rung
it is the named thing itself, existing; naming it is little, and little is not
nothing. What has none of that is a part that asks only to be told something,
where what comes back is an answer about what is already so: nothing there
becomes true, and this column has nothing to measure. A placement offered here
without `becomes` is thrown out, along with the rung it claimed.

**1.** Nothing is closed except which thing it is. They have named what they
want and stopped, so every choice about it — what it contains, how it looks,
how it acts, what it keeps, how it is put together — remains the builder's, and
the builder makes those choices from whatever that name usually means to them.
*Against the rungs below:* nothing has been said about what is in it or what it is like,
so there is nothing to build to except the name. *The mistake to avoid:* naming
something elaborate does not lift a message off this rung — the more a name
already implies, the more the builder is simply reproducing a thing of that
kind from memory, and the less of it is theirs. Adding intensity, urgency, or a
reason why they want it does not lift it either; none of that closes a choice.
*The test:* if two builders acted on this message alone, would they hand back
two recognisably different things? If yes, it belongs here.

**2.** What the thing is or contains is closed; how any of it acts is still
open. They have said what it should be called, what it is made of, what it
looks like, what it says, how many of them there are, or that it should
resemble some existing thing. A builder now knows what to put there and must
still invent everything about what it does. *Against the rung below:* at least one
property or component has been fixed, so two builders would put the same things
there. *Against the rungs below:* a builder would still have to sit down and work out
how the thing behaves before starting. *The mistake to avoid:* stating what the
finished thing should end up like, without saying what brings that about,
belongs here however deep the machinery underneath would have to be —
describing an end state describes the thing, it does not decide how it works.

**3.** The builder is implementing rather than designing. Enough of how it acts
is fixed that the thing can be made the way they meant it without the builder
inventing the decisions that shape it: what happens under which conditions,
what counts as done or as failed, what follows from what, what is allowed and
what is not. *Against the rungs below:* the decisions a builder would otherwise have had
to take have now been taken by them, so the remaining work is carrying them
out. *The mistake to avoid:* one stray rule attached to an otherwise open
request does not reach this rung, and neither does an addition nobody would
have had to decide either way. Size is not the measure — a small thing settled
completely belongs here, and a large thing with a single rule attached does
not.

**4.** Enough of how it acts is fixed that the builder is implementing rather
than designing, AND what persists and whose it is is closed as well. They have
settled what is still there after the moment that produced it, what starts
again and when, whose it is, and who may see it. The builder can no longer
treat this as one person doing one thing once, and has to carry a distinction
it would not have invented on its own. *Against the rung below:* that one fixes
what happens; this one fixes what persists and to whom it belongs. *The mistake to
avoid:* mentioning that other people exist, or that something is saved, does
not reach this rung — what does is fixing a rule about them: whose it is, what
each may see, what survives for whom and what does not.

**5.** Everything the rungs below close is closed, AND the arrangement is closed
too. They have broken something that would not come out in one piece into named
parts and said what each hands to the next, leaving the builder implementing
pieces rather than deciding how the whole fits together. *Against the rung
below:* the ones below settle what the thing does; this one settles how it is
put together to do it. *The mistake to
avoid:* wanting several things at once is not this rung however many are listed
— what makes it this rung is that something passes between the parts, or that
one part is stated to depend on what another produces. Pinning something
unfamiliar to a familiar thing and saying where it differs also belongs here,
because it hands over a structure to work from.

---

**reacting** — the part of a message that says what is currently true of
something that came back. Whatever came back counts, whether or not anything
was built: a result, a plan, an answer, a proposal.

Saying what is true of what came back is this column. Putting it on trial is
not: where what came back was an answer, a message that holds it against
something of their own, or asks whether it still holds, asks to be told whether
the answer holds, and that part of it belongs to the standard that reads what
is asked to be told rather than here.

**1.** They can say that what came back is or is not what they meant, and no
more. Finding out what is wrong with it is entirely the other side's problem:
the message carries a verdict and no location, so the next move is a search.
*Against the rungs below:* nothing in the message narrows where to look. *The mistake to
avoid:* strength of feeling is not information — saying it is completely wrong,
or saying it several times, closes nothing.

Confirming that something is right belongs here too when it says only that:
approval without saying what makes it right leaves the other side unable to tell
what to preserve.

Going on from what came back can carry the same thing a verdict carries: that it
was what they meant. A message that states no verdict in words may still settle
the point by what it does with the result. It meets this step when the request
could not have been written without the result in hand: it names, uses, or
extends something that first appeared in what came back. A next request that
would have read the same way had nothing come back settles nothing about what
came back, and is not placed in this column.

**2.** They name which part is wrong, at the level someone could confirm just
by looking rather than by investigating. The other side now knows where to go,
and must still work out why it is wrong and what to change. *Against the rung below:* a place has been named. *Against the rungs below:* a
diagnosis is still needed after this message. *The mistake to avoid:*
describing the effect they noticed rather than the part producing it stays at
rung 1 — the report has to point at something, not at how it felt.

**3.** They name which rule is wrong rather than which part: what it does under
some condition that it should not, what number it arrives at that is wrong,
what it fails to do when it ought to. The other side can change something
without investigating first. *Against the rungs below:* after a rung-2 report somebody
still has to diagnose; after this one they only have to fix. *The mistake to
avoid:* naming a broken part in technical language is still rung 2 — what lifts
a report here is that the behaviour, not the location, is what has been
identified.

**4.** They separate what is held from what is produced out of it, or say whose
copy is at fault: that the thing kept is wrong rather than what is shown from
it, or that it is wrong for one party and right for another. *Against the rungs below:*
rung 3 identifies a behaviour; this rung identifies which layer that behaviour
lives in. *The mistake to avoid:* mentioning where something is stored is not
enough — what makes it this rung is the distinction being drawn, so that the
other side knows which of two places to open. It takes knowing that there are
two.

**5.** They locate the fault at one of the parts they themselves set out
earlier, inside an arrangement they supplied. The other side is not being told
where something is wrong in general, but which piece of a structure this person
defined is the one misbehaving. *Against the rungs below:* this rung presupposes that
they gave the structure in the first place, so it can only follow their own
earlier rung-5 asking. *The mistake to avoid:* naming a part of a structure
somebody else supplied is rung 2 or 3 — the structure has to be theirs for this
to be evidence about them.

---

**blocked** — the part of a message that says progress has stopped.

**1.** They come at it again and add nothing the other side did not already
have: the same request in the same words, or reworded with no new information,
or a statement that it is still not working and nothing further. *Against rung
2:* nothing has been said about the circumstances in which it fails. *The
mistake to avoid:* what puts a message here is not that it repeats — it is that
the other side is no better off than before. A repetition that adds one new
fact is not this rung; an elaborate new phrasing of the same content is.

**2.** They say what was being done when it stopped, or on which part it
stopped, so that it can be found again. The other side can reproduce the
trouble instead of hunting for it, which is usually the expensive half of
fixing it. *Against the rung below:* the message contains something that narrows where
to look. *Against the rungs below:* what should have happened has not been said, so
success still cannot be told from failure without asking. *The mistake to
avoid:* saying that it never works, or always fails, adds no circumstance and
stays at rung 1.

**3.** They say what should have happened instead of what did. The other side
can now tell success from failure on its own, because the intended behaviour
has been stated rather than assumed. *Against the rungs below:* rung 2 says where to
look, this rung says what to look for. *The mistake to avoid:* restating the
original request is not saying what should have happened — what makes this rung
is that the expectation is given in a form that can be checked against the
result, so that someone else could confirm the fix without asking them.

**4.** They say which data, or whose state, the trouble is in, narrowing it to
one side of a boundary so that the other side can stop searching everywhere
else. *Against the rungs below:* rung 3 says what the right behaviour would be; this
rung says where the wrong one is coming from. *The mistake to avoid:* naming
any two-way split does not reach this rung — it has to be a claim about where
the trouble lives, made with some reason to believe it, not a guess offered
among others. It takes a picture of where things are kept rather than only of
what is happening on the surface.

**5.** They change the approach rather than the wording: dropping to a smaller
or more literal description, or cutting one piece off to settle on its own
first, handing over something that can actually be finished. *Against the rungs below:*
the lower rungs describe the trouble better; this one changes what is being
asked for so that the trouble stops mattering. *The mistake to avoid:*
abandoning the goal is not this rung, and neither is asking the other side what
to do — what makes it this rung is that they themselves have diagnosed their
own request as the obstacle and restructured it, and the restructured version
is in the message.

---

There is a sixth rung in each column, and it is what the whole ladder is
climbing towards: **not rebuilding what the assistant already knows.** Leaning
on what it has seen as leverage, spending specification only on what it has
not, and breaking that down structurally until every piece is again something
it knows. Whoever can do this can get built anything whose logic they
themselves understand. It shows only across many tasks, never inside one, and
is read elsewhere. **Never place a message above 5.**

## Credit only what they decided

Take each thing a message appears to settle and ask: could they have said the
opposite, and still have been naming the same thing? If not, they did not
decide it — it came with the noun.

Most of what a name appears to promise is carried by the name itself: whatever
a competent builder would supply unprompted on hearing the name was not decided
by the requester. Ask that of the name in front of you, never against a list. A
message whose whole content is the name of what is wanted is rung 1, however
much the named thing would involve.

The test is the opposite: state it the other way round and see whether it is
still the same thing they asked for. If it is, they decided something. If
saying the opposite would make it a different thing, or nonsense, the name was
doing the work and they decided nothing.

## Only their words count

What came back is shown so you can tell what they were responding to and
whether their words landed. **What the assistant said, proposed or built is
never theirs.** If a decision appears in what came back but not in what they
said, they did not make it and it earns them nothing. Every rung above 1 must
carry `cite`: a fragment copied out of THEIR message.
It is checked against that message, ignoring case and spacing; a fragment that is not in it is thrown out
along with the placement it supported.

A decision already made in what came back is not made again by repeating it.
Before crediting a rung, ask whether the message would still settle that point
had the previous answer never said it. A word they introduced themselves stays
theirs however often it has been echoed back since.

## Did it land

Every placement also carries `landed`, read from what came back after that
message:

`yes` — what came back was what the message called for. `no` — it was not, and
words they could have chosen would have made the difference. Ask it that way
round: is there a wording of theirs that would have got this? `not_theirs` — it
was not, and no wording of theirs would have changed this attempt. This counts
neither way. Use it on evidence in what came back, never to avoid a hard call.

**Being asked to clarify is not `not_theirs` — it is `no`.** If what came back is
a question about what they meant, or a choice made on their behalf between
readings they left open, then their words were not enough to act on, and words
they could have chosen would have been. That is the clearest evidence there is
that the message fell short, and it is the opposite of a reason to set it
aside. `not_theirs` is for what no wording could have reached: a refusal, a limit
of the environment, a failure with a cause of its own.

A message they had to send again, in the same column, about the same thing, did
not land the first time: saying it well enough that it happens is the whole of
what is being measured here.

## What is not on the ladder

A message may carry nothing, and an empty answer is expected rather than a
failure: every part of it asks for something to be told to them rather than for
something to become true, or it is about the exchange rather than about the
work.

A part that asks only to be told something is the case where **asking** has
nothing to measure: it carries no `becomes` and is not placed in that column. A telling
that lands is still a telling, and whether the answer was correct, useful or
complete has no bearing on it. Where one message does both, `becomes` is about
the part that would make something so, and the part that asks to be told is
simply not what that placement is for.

Name those messages rather than only leaving them out. Asked one question —
where does this sit — a reader finds somewhere for everything, and a message
that asks to be told something arrives on this ladder at its lowest rung, where
it reads as somebody who named what they wanted and stopped. So there is a
second answer to give, and it is asked of every message separately from the
first: `elsewhere` carries the messages whose WHOLE ask is to be told
something. Every entry carries `cite`, a fragment copied out of THAT message —
the words that ask to be told — checked the way a placement's is; an entry
whose fragment is not in the message it names is dropped, and whatever it would
have taken off the ladder stays there. A message named there is placed in no column here. A message that also
carries something these three columns measure is placed for that part, and is
not named there.

Some messages arrive already marked as not having been input at all — never an
attempt to say anything. That mark is made by a step that read the conversation and not the ladder, and
it says the message is there as circumstance: it stays in its place, and what
follows it often only makes sense because it happened, but nothing in it was
theirs to decide. Nothing is placed for it, and it is named in `elsewhere` no
more than it is placed: it belongs to no ladder at all.

That is a different thing from a message that was read and was not enough.
Being asked which of several things they meant is being read; so is a fix that
came back wrong. Those are theirs and they belong on the ladder.

Judge by what a message contains, never by what kind of sentence it is. A
question can report that progress has stopped, or carry an expectation about
what came back. An instruction about the work can carry a requirement inside
it.

Difficulty has no bearing anywhere. A vast thing the assistant has built a
thousand times comes out in one go, and a rule it has never seen goes wrong in
thirty lines — how hard a person would find it is an opinion about the work and
none of it is theirs. Neither does anything about them as a person, nor how
long the message is.

## Also, for the task as a whole

**outcome** — `done` or `abandoned`: whether this objective was settled or
dropped. Read it from the record, never from how long they persisted; coming
back across days is a separate note and is no part of the ladder.

**familiarity** — `known` or `unseen`: could the assistant have done this from
the name alone, or has it plainly never seen anything like it. Never about how
much work it would be, and never about how much they had to decide.
