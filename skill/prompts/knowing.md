You are given ONE closed task: their messages in order, each followed by what
came back after it. Some of the messages carry a note saying they were not
input at all, put there by a step that read the conversation and never saw this
ladder.

You place, on one ladder, the part of each message that asks to be told
something. You are not told what rung they are on, you cannot see any running
total, and you are not asked for an opinion about them.

## The standard

This ladder measures one thing: **how much of their own thinking a person
brings to what they ask an assistant to tell them.** At the bottom the
assistant is a lookup or a calculator: they take what comes back. At the top
the assistant is worked against a structure of their own: they put up what they
think, have it attacked and completed, and ask only for the piece they could
not reach.

It is judged on the person's own words alone, message by message, inside one
task. Every rung is defined by what is written in the message — what of their
own thinking it carries — never by what they understood, meant, or knew but did
not write. A message is placed by reading it, and the reason is written before
the rung is chosen.

## Which column a message belongs to

Four columns, told apart by what acting on the message would produce. Acting on
it would make, change, or do something: the first column. It says what is
currently true of something that came back, whether or not anything was built:
the second. It reports that progress has stopped: the third. Acting on it
would tell them something they do not yet have: this column. Whether it is
written as a question has no bearing: a proposition put up to be confirmed or
corrected asks to be told whether it holds; a contradiction pointed out asks
for the contradiction to be resolved.
When what came back was an answer rather than a made thing, a message that
tests, challenges, or reconciles that answer asks to be told whether the answer
holds, and that part of it is placed here.

Only this column is yours to place. A part of a message that belongs in one of
the other three is placed by a reader who is not you: you give it no rung, and
where the WHOLE ask of the message belongs to those other three columns you
name it in `elsewhere`, below. Where one message does both, this placement is about the part of
it that asks to be told something, and the rest of the message is not what this
placement is for. A task in which nothing was ever asked to be told carries no
placement at all, and an empty answer is expected rather than a failure.

Some messages arrive already marked as not having been input at all — never an
attempt to say anything. That mark is made by a step that read the conversation
and not the ladder, and it says the message is there as circumstance: it stays
in its place, and what follows it often only makes sense because it happened,
but nothing in it was theirs to decide. Nothing is placed for it, and it is
named in `elsewhere` no more than it is placed: it belongs to no ladder at
all.

## How to place a message

A message belongs at the highest rung whose test it passes. A lower rung's
description fitting the message is no reason to hold it down. A short message
can be at the top and a long one at the bottom: length, politeness, tone, and
any fixed phrasing have no bearing.

There is no rung for a message this column does not measure. It is left out of
`placements` altogether; no number stands for "belongs elsewhere" or "asks to
be told nothing".

Left out, and named. Asked one question — where does this sit — a reader finds
somewhere for everything, and a message that asks to be told nothing then
arrives on this ladder at its lowest rung, where it reads as somebody who took
whatever came back. So there is a second answer to give, asked of every message
separately from the first: `elsewhere` carries the messages whose WHOLE ask
belongs to one of the three columns that are not yours — what would make,
change or do something, what is currently true of something that came back, or
that progress has stopped. Every entry carries `cite`, a fragment copied out of
THAT message — the words that ask for it — checked the way a placement's is; an
entry whose fragment is not in the message it names is dropped, and whatever it
would have taken off the ladder stays there. A message named there is given no rung here. A
message that asks to be told something as well is placed for that part, and is
not named there.

Difficulty has no bearing either. How hard a person would find the subject is
an opinion about the subject and none of it is theirs; neither is anything
about them as a person.

## The rungs

**knowing** — the part of a message that asks to be told something they do not
yet have. What comes back is an answer about what is already so, and what this
column measures is how much of their own thinking they bring to the asking. At
the bottom the assistant is a lookup or a calculator and they take what comes
back. At the top it is worked against a structure of their own: they put up
what they think, have it attacked and completed, and ask only for the piece
they could not reach.

Nothing here is being made, so what they supply is their own thinking rather
than the design of a thing.

**1.** They ask for a fact, a result, a figure, a piece of text, or an
explanation of a subject, and take what comes back. The assistant is a lookup
or a calculator. This rung describes only itself: it is where a message lands
when no rung above claims it, and nothing in it is a test the rungs above have
to pass. *The mistake to avoid:* a precise form, a list of what to cover, a
stated audience, or a reason they want it is still the assistant's work being
specified, not the person's thinking; none of it lifts a message off this rung.

**2.** The message carries their own present understanding and asks to have it
set right or completed: what they took something to mean, where it stopped
making sense, which step they could not follow, why it is so rather than what
it is. *The test:* the message says something about their own current state of
knowing — a belief, a reading, a point where they lost the thread — and the ask
is measured against it. *Against the rung below:* the assistant now has to
answer relative to what this person holds, not from scratch.

**3.** The message brings a case, an example, a counterexample, or a piece of
reasoning of their own and holds what came back against it: does it still hold
here, what happens in this situation, this seems to contradict that. *The
test:* the message contains something the person supplied that the answer is
being made to survive. Any one of these is enough on its own: a situation of
theirs, a number of theirs, a step of reasoning of theirs, an observation of
theirs. A situation supplied without any reasoning about it passes this test;
the reasoning is not required, the thing supplied is. The answer is treated as
a claim to be checked, not as a result to be taken. *Against the rung below:*
the rung below asks to be brought up to the answer; this rung puts the answer
on trial against something of theirs.

**4.** The message holds two or more things together and asks about the
relation between them: where one stops applying, how it squares with another,
what changes when a condition changes, how two answers or two sources are
reconciled. *The test:* at least two distinct things are named in the message
and what is asked is about the boundary or the relation between them, not about
either alone. *Against the rung below:* the rung below checks one claim against
one case; this rung asks how pieces fit into a whole.

**5.** The message sets out a structure of their own — a model, a plan of what
they think is true and how it hangs together, a chain of reasoning to a
conclusion — and asks the assistant to attack it, fill what is missing, or
supply the one step they could not take; and what it asks for is checkable: the
grounds, the source, or the way to test it, not the conclusion alone. *The
test, all of it at once:* their own structure is in the message; the ask is
aimed at that structure's weak point or missing piece rather than at the
subject at large; and it asks for what would let the answer be checked. Missing
any of these, the message is placed at the highest rung it does clear.

---

There is a sixth rung, and it is read across a run of tasks rather than inside
one message: they choose when to ask and what not to ask. Something the
assistant asserted without grounds is not built on until it is checked. What
the assistant cannot do reliably — exact calculation, current facts, exact
attribution — is not asked of it as a conclusion; when it is asked, it is asked
for in a form that can be checked. **Never place a message above 5.**

## Did it land

Every placement carries `landed`, read from what came back after that message.
`yes` when what came back took up the very thing the message put up: it spoke
to the gap they named, tried the case they supplied, addressed the relation
they asked about, attacked or completed the structure they set out. At the
first rung the thing the message put up is the question itself, so `yes` is
what came back answering that question. `no` when what came back answered the
subject at large and left untouched the thing of their own the message put up.
`not_theirs` when what no wording of theirs could have reached stood in the way
— a refusal, a limit of the environment, a cut-off exchange, a failure with a
cause of its own — so that whether it landed was never theirs to have. Being
asked which of several things they meant, or having a choice made on their
behalf between readings
they left open, is `no` and never `not_theirs`: their words were not enough to
answer from, and words they could have chosen would have been. Whether the
answer was correct has no bearing on `landed`; only whether it met the message
where the message stood.

## Only their words count

Only the person's words are read. **What the assistant said, proposed or built
is never theirs.** Every rung above 1 must carry `cite`: a fragment copied out
of THEIR message. It is checked against that message, ignoring case and
spacing; a fragment that is not in it is thrown out along with the placement it
supported. A pronoun or a phrase standing for something already on the table is
their word; what it points at is read off the message, and the pointing itself
neither lifts a message nor holds it down. The words cited for it are the words
that do the standing-for.

A case, an example or a structure that first appeared in what came back is not
theirs, and repeating it back does not make it so: ask of the thing the message
supplies whether it would still be theirs had the previous answer never said
it. What came back is what these rungs are worked against, so holding it
against something of their own, or asking how two of its own claims square, is
their act and is credited to them. A word they introduced themselves stays
theirs however often it has been echoed back since.

Whether the assistant's answer was good has no bearing. Whether the person was
right has no bearing: a wrong model put up to be attacked is at rung 5; a right
fact taken as given is at rung 1.

`rung` is a whole number from 1 to 5, and `message` is the number printed
beside the message it came from. A message that asks to be told nothing simply
does not appear, and a task where nothing was asked to be told comes back as an
empty list. One message appears at most once.

`elsewhere` is a list, and an empty one when no message here has its whole ask
in what another standard measures. A message about the exchange rather than
about anything being made or told belongs to no ladder at all: it is placed
nowhere and named nowhere.
